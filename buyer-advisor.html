INDEPENDENT US BUYING RESEARCH

Build a better buying brief.

Before comparing products, write down the requirements that would make a purchase useful for your household, work or travel. This page is a practical checklist, not an automated product recommendation or a live pricing tool.

1. Define the job the product must do

Describe your primary use case in one sentence. For example: “I need reliable video calls in a noisy shared office,” rather than “I want the best headphones.” Then list two deal-breakers such as microphone clarity, comfort or compatibility.

2. Set a complete US budget

Include sales tax where applicable, accessories, replacement parts, subscription fees and likely maintenance. A lower advertised price can lead to a higher total cost. Use our ownership cost calculator to compare scenarios with your own figures.

3. Verify compatibility before comparing brands

Check device dimensions, connections, operating-system support, voltage, Wi-Fi requirements or sizing as relevant. Use the manufacturer's specifications and the retailer's current listing rather than assuming two similar-looking models are interchangeable.

4. Compare three realistic choices

Shortlist a budget option, a balanced option and a premium option. Score each against your own priorities in the Decision Lab. Do not treat illustrative scores as independent product tests.

5. Check the purchase terms

Before ordering, verify seller identity, current price, return window, warranty coverage, shipping costs and whether the item is sold or fulfilled by a third party. US state and local taxes vary.
