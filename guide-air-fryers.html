Capacity should match the food, not only household size Basket volume is useful, but usable cooking area matters more for foods that need a single layer. A larger family may benefit from a wide basket or dual-zone design. Measure counter and storage space before choosing an oversized model. Single basket versus dual zone A single large basket is simple and flexible for one main dish. Dual-zone models can cook two foods with different times or temperatures, but each compartment may be narrow. Think about the meals you actually prepare rather than assuming more zones are automatically better. Controls and temperature range Preset buttons are convenient, but accurate manual time and temperature controls matter more. Check the minimum and maximum temperature if you plan to reheat, dehydrate or bake. Clear controls are especially useful when several people use the appliance. Cleaning and coatings Removable dishwasher-safe parts can reduce friction, but manufacturer instructions should be followed to protect coatings. Look at basket corners, mesh plates and heating-area access. A difficult-to-clean appliance is less likely to be used regularly. Noise, heat and placement Air fryers move hot air quickly and can produce fan noise and exhaust heat. Follow clearance guidance and avoid blocking vents. Check cord length and outlet location before deciding where the appliance will live. What an air fryer will not do It is essentially a compact convection appliance, not a magic replacement for every oven or frying method. Results depend on food quantity, spacing and recipe. Buy it for convenience and fast small-batch cooking, not because of exaggerated universal claims. A simple decision process Write down your primary use case and the one limitation you most want to avoid. Set a realistic budget including accessories or consumables. Compare three to five candidates on the criteria above rather than on feature count. Verify current seller, warranty, return terms, delivery and price on the retailer page. Choose only after confirming the product fits your actual routine. Questions to ask before checkout Which feature will I use every week? What compatibility or size constraint could make this purchase fail? What recurring costs or replacement parts should I expect? Can I return it easily if fit or performance is wrong? Am I paying for a specification that does not improve my use case? Bottom line There is no single best air fryer for everyone. The useful choice is the one that fits your use case, constraints and ownership expectations. Basket capacity vs serving size Compare the usable cooking surface, not just the advertised litre figure. For two foods cooked together, check whether a divider reduces space and whether each side can be controlled separately.
