Retailer availability does not determine the criteria in this guide. Airline limits come first Carry-on size and weight limits vary by airline and fare. Check the current rules for the airlines you actually use before buying. External dimensions should include wheels and handles when the airline measures the whole bag. Hard shell versus soft side Hard shells resist compression and can handle rain better, while soft-sided bags may offer exterior pockets and flexible packing. Neither is universally superior. Consider your packing style and how often you need quick access to documents or electronics. Wheels and handles Smooth wheels and a rigid telescoping handle affect every airport transfer. Spinner wheels are maneuverable, while two-wheel designs can provide more internal space and handle rough surfaces differently. Check replacement-part availability for premium luggage. Interior organization Compression panels, dividers and pockets can keep small loads stable, but excessive internal hardware consumes space and adds weight. Travelers who use packing cubes may prefer a simpler interior. Empty weight matters A heavy suitcase uses part of the airline weight allowance before you pack anything. Lightweight construction is useful, but extremely thin materials may trade durability for weight. Compare the balance that fits your travel frequency. Warranty and repair Wheels, handles and zippers are common wear points. Read what the warranty actually covers and whether repairs are available where you live. A long warranty is more valuable when the service process is practical. A simple decision process Write down your primary use case and the one limitation you most want to avoid. Set a realistic budget including accessories or consumables. Compare three to five candidates on the criteria above rather than on feature count. Verify current seller, warranty, return terms, delivery and price on the retailer page. Choose only after confirming the product fits your actual routine. Questions to ask before checkout Which feature will I use every week? What compatibility or size constraint could make this purchase fail? What recurring costs or replacement parts should I expect? Can I return it easily if fit or performance is wrong? Am I paying for a specification that does not improve my use case? Bottom line There is no single best carry-on luggage for everyone. The useful choice is the one that fits your use case, constraints and ownership expectations.
