Retailer availability does not determine the criteria in this guide. Start with viewing distance and desk size Screen size and resolution should work together. A very large display can feel overwhelming on a shallow desk, while low pixel density can make text look coarse. Measure available width, depth and stand footprint before shopping. Resolution and scaling Higher resolution provides more workspace and sharper text, but it also demands more graphics performance in games and may require operating-system scaling. For productivity, text clarity and usable workspace often matter more than headline refresh rate. Refresh rate and response behavior High refresh rates make motion smoother, especially in games, but response-time marketing can be inconsistent. Look for independent motion testing and overshoot behavior rather than relying on the smallest advertised millisecond number. Color and brightness Creators should care about color-space coverage, calibration behavior and panel uniformity. Office users may prioritize comfortable brightness control and anti-glare treatment. HDR labels vary widely and do not guarantee a strong HDR experience. Ports and docking USB-C monitors can simplify laptop setups when they provide enough charging power and support video/data. Check power delivery, USB hub behavior and whether the monitor has the ports required by your computer and peripherals. Ergonomics and mounting Height adjustment, tilt, swivel and VESA mounting can improve long-term usability. A good panel on a poor stand may still require an additional monitor arm, which should be included in the total budget. A simple decision process Write down your primary use case and the one limitation you most want to avoid. Set a realistic budget including accessories or consumables. Compare three to five candidates on the criteria above rather than on feature count. Verify current seller, warranty, return terms, delivery and price on the retailer page. Choose only after confirming the product fits your actual routine. Questions to ask before checkout Which feature will I use every week? What compatibility or size constraint could make this purchase fail? What recurring costs or replacement parts should I expect? Can I return it easily if fit or performance is wrong? Am I paying for a specification that does not improve my use case? Bottom line There is no single best computer monitor for everyone. The useful choice is the one that fits your use case, constraints and ownership expectations. Match panel to working distance Confirm native resolution, physical screen size and scaling support. Verify the exact cable and port combination needed for the desired refresh rate; not every HDMI or USB-C connection supports every mode.
