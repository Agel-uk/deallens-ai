Start with the device, not the capacity number A phone, tablet, handheld console and laptop have different charging requirements. First check the maximum input your device can accept and whether it uses USB Power Delivery or another standard. A large battery with weak output can still be a poor match for a laptop. Understand usable capacity The capacity printed in mAh is measured at the internal cell voltage, so the energy delivered to a phone is lower after voltage conversion and normal efficiency losses. Treat capacity as a comparison tool rather than a promise of exact full charges. Output power and ports USB-C ports may support different input and output limits even on the same power bank. If you charge two devices at once, total power may be shared. Check the multi-port output table rather than assuming every port can deliver the advertised maximum simultaneously. Travel and airline considerations Airline rules are generally based on watt-hours, not just mAh, and restrictions can vary. Before flying, verify the current airline and aviation authority rules for your route. A clearly labeled watt-hour rating can make checks easier. Heat, cables and charging time Fast charging creates heat, and poor cables can limit performance. Use a cable rated for the required power. Also check how quickly the power bank itself recharges; a large pack that takes all night to refill may not suit frequent travel. Who should avoid oversized models If you only need an emergency phone top-up, a very large pack adds weight without much benefit. Laptop users may justify the extra capacity and higher USB-C output. Match size to the longest period you realistically spend away from a charger. mAh vs Wh: compare energy correctly Milliamp-hours (mAh) describe charge at a stated voltage; watt-hours (Wh) describe energy. When the cell voltage is known, an approximate conversion is Wh = (mAh × volts) ÷ 1,000. For example, a 20,000 mAh pack rated at 3.7 V stores about 74 Wh before conversion losses. The exact value depends on the manufacturer’s rated voltage and specifications. Do not divide a power bank’s advertised mAh by your phone’s battery mAh and assume that many full charges. Voltage conversion, cable losses, heat and battery-management overhead reduce delivered energy. Check USB-C wattage before buying Read the output specification for the specific USB-C port you will use. For a laptop, compare the power bank’s supported USB Power Delivery output and voltage profiles with the laptop’s charging requirements. A pack that supports only modest output may maintain battery level during light use but struggle under heavier workloads. If you plan to charge multiple devices, look for the manufacturer’s simultaneous-output ratings. A headline maximum wattage may apply only when one port is used. Check whether the USB-C port also supports input charging and how long it takes to refill the pack. Travel checklist for U.S. shoppers Airlines and aviation authorities generally assess lithium-ion battery limits in watt-hours. Spare lithium batteries and power banks are generally carried in cabin baggage rather than checked baggage, subject to current airline and regulator requirements. Policies and approval requirements can vary by battery rating, carrier and itinerary. Verify the latest guidance with your airline and the FAA PackSafe lithium-battery guidance before travel. Choose a product with a legible capacity and Wh label, protect it from damage and short circuits, and follow airline instructions on use or charging onboard. Five checks before checkout Identify whether you need emergency phone power, daily multi-device charging or laptop charging. Check the required USB-C charging standard, wattage and compatible cable. Compare rated Wh, pack weight and expected practical usage—not advertised mAh alone. Check each port’s output when more than one device is connected, plus recharge time for the pack itself. Verify seller, warranty, returns, current price and any airline restrictions on the retailer and carrier websites. Common questions Is a 20,000 mAh power bank enough for a laptop? Capacity alone cannot answer this. Check its USB-C Power Delivery wattage and your laptop’s input requirements; some laptops need more output than phone-oriented packs provide. Does a higher wattage damage my phone? With properly implemented compatible charging standards, the device negotiates the power it draws. Choose reputable, standards-compliant products and appropriate cables rather than relying on wattage claims alone. Can I bring a power bank on a plane? Many are permitted in carry-on baggage under applicable limits, but restrictions depend on Wh rating and the airline. Check current rules before flying. Bottom line The best power bank is the one whose output, capacity, ports and size match your devices and routine.
