Power Bank Buying Guide

A useful power bank is one that matches your devices, charging speed and travel needs. Capacity alone does not tell you whether it will charge a laptop, fast-charge a phone, or remain convenient to carry.

Capacity versus usable energy

Advertised milliamp-hours are a cell rating, not the exact energy delivered to your phone. Conversion losses are normal. Choose capacity based on the number of top-ups you need and the size and weight you are willing to carry.

USB-C Power Delivery

For modern phones, tablets and many laptops, check the USB-C PD output in watts. A bank can have a large capacity but a low output. Laptop users should compare the required wattage of their device with the bank's maximum USB-C output.

Ports and simultaneous charging

Check how power is divided when more than one port is used. Some models reduce output substantially with two devices connected. If you carry multiple devices, look for clearly documented per-port and combined limits.

Travel and safety

Airline rules commonly focus on watt-hours and may require power banks to remain in carry-on baggage. Always verify the current airline and airport rules before travel. Prefer established products with clear safety certifications and warranty support.

Common mistakes

Avoid choosing only by the biggest mAh number. Also check dimensions, weight, recharge time, cable requirements and whether the advertised fast-charge standard matches your devices.
