A five-step buying framework.

DealLens is most useful before you start comparing brands. The goal is to turn a vague purchase into a short list of requirements you can verify.

1. Define the job

Describe what the product needs to do in your real routine. Include where, how often and for how long you expect to use it.

2. Identify failure points

Write down what would make the purchase disappointing: poor fit, incompatible ports, difficult cleaning, short battery life, unavailable replacement parts or restrictive returns.

3. Separate needs from extras

Choose a few must-have criteria and treat everything else as optional. This makes it easier to resist feature lists that add cost without improving your use case.

4. Compare ownership, not only purchase price

Consider consumables, accessories, subscriptions, maintenance, repair options and expected replacement cycles.

5. Verify current commercial details

After narrowing the category, verify current price, seller, warranty, delivery, stock and return terms on the retailer page. These details change too quickly to treat a static guide as the source of truth.

What DealLens does not do

We do not invent live prices, claim testing we did not perform, or rank products simply because an affiliate link exists. Our purpose is to make the decision criteria clearer before you shop.
