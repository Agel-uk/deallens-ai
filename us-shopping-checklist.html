Worked example: choosing a cordless drill

Scenario: You need a drill for occasional furniture assembly and light household repairs. Kit A is $89 and Kit B is $119 before tax.

Kit A includes one battery but its replacement battery costs $55. Kit B includes two compatible batteries. If you expect to need a second battery, the comparable pre-tax cost becomes $144 for A versus $119 for B. The cheaper sticker price is not automatically the cheaper kit.

These are hypothetical teaching figures, not observed retailer offers. Check torque settings, chuck size, weight, battery ecosystem, return terms, and warranty before deciding.

For drill-specific buying criteria, see the cordless drill guide. To compare costs over several years, use the ownership cost calculator.

What this checklist cannot verify

Policies vary by seller, product category, state, and purchase date. Sales taxes are location-dependent. Manufacturer warranty eligibility can depend on the specific seller and product. Always confirm the actual listing and written policies immediately before paying.
